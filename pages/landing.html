What we do

Four questions, four methods

Most briefs arrive as a topic. The first thing we do is turn it into a question a method can answer. These are the four we are asked most often.

What does this audience already believe?

Exploratory qualitative — online overtime boards or live groups — to map existing attitudes before anyone writes a word of campaign copy.

Online boards, live focus groups

Which of our messages actually lands?

Message testing against annotated verbatims, so you can see the exact clause that turned people, not just a score.

Narrative testing, frame comparison

What do the people who decide think?

In-depth interviews with stakeholders — parliamentary, media, sector — attributed simply, never named.

Stakeholder interviews, sector consultation

How many people hold this view?

National polling to size an attitude you already understand, with the question wording and base reported on every chart.

Essential poll, standalone survey

How we work

Every project runs the same shape

The scale changes. The sequence does not. You will always know which stage you are in and what comes out of it.

Stage 1
Initial snapshot

A short quantitative read to establish where the population sits before we spend money on qualitative time.

You get: a sizing of the problem and a recruitment screener that reflects it.

Stage 2
Exploratory qualitative

Groups or an online board with the cohorts who will decide the outcome, including the ones who disagree with you.

You get: the language your audience uses, and the objections you have not planned for.

Stage 3
Iterative testing

Narratives put in front of people and revised between sessions rather than after the project.

You get: annotated verbatims showing which clause worked and which one lost the room.

Stage 4
Quantitative validation

A standalone poll to confirm the qualitative reading holds at scale and to size each segment.

You get: defensible numbers, with the question wording and base attached to every one.

What you get

A report you can hand to a minister

Every deliverable follows the same rules, and they are not stylistic:

  • One finding per slide. If the title cannot summarise the slide, it is two slides.
  • The finding is in the title. Not the topic. You should be able to read the contents page and know the argument.
  • Quotes support, never introduce. A verbatim carries a finding the analysis has already stated.
  • Every chart carries its working. The question exactly as fielded, the base, and the n. Where labels are suppressed, we say so.
  • Hedges are calibrated. Most, many, some, a few — each means something specific, and we do not upgrade one to make a finding sound stronger.
