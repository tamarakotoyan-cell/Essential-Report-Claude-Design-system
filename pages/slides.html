methodology-phases

Research Design

Phase 1

Public

What?

Three-day online overtime discussion board with n=38 participants.

Why?

Exploratory qualitative research into existing attitudes, and into public support for more funding.

Who?

50/50 male and female. Mix of ages, states, incomes and household types.

Phase 2

Stakeholders

What?

Nine in-depth interviews of 45 to 60 minutes with people working in government or the legal sector.

Why?

To get feedback on the narrative frames and messages.

Who?

A mix of executive, political advisory and media advisory positions.

Phase 3

Refinement

What?

One-day online board with nine stakeholders reviewing the refined messages.

Why?

To understand how to optimise each message for government and for the public.

Who?

Communications and leadership roles across the eight commissions.

finding-analysis-2quotes — the default finding slide

'Legal aid' has an extremely strong brand among the public and is seen to cover all elements of legal representation

Legal Aid has strong brand and name recognition.

  • Almost everyone says they have heard of Legal Aid and knows that it provides free legal advice and assistance across a wide range of legal matters.
  • While they might be conflating Legal Aid with other community legal centres, this helps demonstrate the leadership position it holds in the sector.

There is an assumption that if someone needs Legal Aid, they will get it.

  • The public doesn't have a deep understanding of how and who gets to access Legal Aid.
  • There is low awareness of the means testing, and this results in many assuming the service is available for anyone unable to afford private legal fees.

testing-positive-improve

Fairness: Legal Aid stakeholders prefer the fairness frame as it is relatable to the public and highlights the scale of the problem

Positive
  • Stakeholders believe this approach will gain traction with the public and resonate strongly because fairness is a core part of the Australian identity.
  • The idea that access to justice is currently a privilege of the rich feels credible and concerning.
  • The phrase “justice delayed is justice denied” is powerful and memorable.
Improve
  • Some say to consider flipping the eight per cent statistic to put emphasis on the limitation.
  • Some mention avoiding the implication that lawyers are motivated by money.

There are too many steps needed to link coal, oil and gas companies to climate disasters — the connection feels too indirect

Polluters contribute to climate change
Climate change results in more disasters
Disasters damage communities
Government and insurers fund the rebuild
Households cover it through levies and premiums

Few soft supporters made these connections unprompted. The campaign has to draw the link between the companies and the cost, and explain why they should be the ones to pay.
